# Advance voting, mail voting, and the election-day forecast target

**Research date:** 2026-08-17

**Question:** What is known, and when, about ballots cast before Toronto election day; do Toronto polls distinguish people who have already voted; and what factual constraints follow for an election-day valid-vote-share forecast at an explicit Analysis Cutoff?

**Scope:** Toronto mayoral and council general elections and by-elections from 2003 through the planned 2026 general election. This is an evidence and data-boundary note. It does not select a model, an update rule, or a publication policy.

## Executive findings

1. **A material share of Toronto ballots is now cast before election day.** Advance voting grew from about 6% of reported turnout in 2003 to about 16% in 2014 and 2018. In 2022, the City's final administrative report assigns 115,899 ballots to advance voting and 19,926 to mail voting: together, 24.1% of the reported 563,124 ballots. In the 2023 mayoral by-election, the reported advance and mail figures amount to 21.8% of reported turnout.

2. **Turnout can become observable while vote choice remains secret.** Recent City practice has been to publish an aggregate advance-vote turnout shortly after advance voting closes. The 2022 citywide count was published the next day; the 2023 mayoral count was published the next day and a ward breakdown followed one day later. Recent council by-elections show the same pattern. Those releases identify how many people voted, not whom they supported.

3. **The 2026 rules make the information asymmetry explicit.** Advance voting is October 6–11, mail ballots must arrive by noon October 14, and election day is October 26. Candidates can obtain a list of people who voted after each advance-vote day; the date for the corresponding mail-voter list is still “to be determined.” Advance-vote results are not transmitted until after 8 p.m. on election night. Mail ballots may be processed and fed through a tabulator earlier, but the tabulator is shut down without generating results; results are generated only after election-day voting closes ([2026 key dates](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/elections/key-dates/), [candidate portal and result procedure](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/elections/candidates-third-party-advertisers/candidate-information/manage-campaign/), [mail-voting procedure](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/8eb5-Mail-in-voting-programFinalApril-2026-combined.pdf)).

4. **Historical cutoff reconstruction cannot use the final mode totals as if they were known contemporaneously.** The public 2022 advance count was 115,911 on October 15; the later election report says 115,899. Similar small revisions occurred in the 2023 Ward 20 and 2024 Ward 15 by-elections. For 2003–2018, the retained official sources establish post-election totals but not a dated pre-election public release. A historical fold therefore needs dated cutoff evidence, not a backfilled final voter-statistics workbook.

5. **The public Toronto poll materials reviewed do not cleanly separate already-cast votes.** Ipsos fielded during advance voting in 2023 but offered only prospective turnout categories and asked who respondents “would” vote for. Liaison polled after advance and mail voting had closed and explicitly acknowledged that those ballots were already cast, but its public release still reported one all-voter topline and one decided-voter topline. Neither published a separate candidate distribution for already-voted respondents. A 2014 Ipsos instrument shows the same prospective wording after advance voting had ended.

6. **“Ballots cast” is not automatically the forecast denominator.** The target is candidate valid-vote share. Administrative turnout can include rejected, declined, blank, voided, or otherwise uncounted ballots. The City's own 2023 mayoral report says 725,333 ballots were cast, but its three mode figures sum to 724,638—exactly 695 fewer—and the certified candidate totals also sum to 724,638. Historical ingestion must preserve the source's count meaning rather than silently equating turnout with valid candidate votes.

Toronto first offered mail voting in the January 2021 Ward 22 council by-election and first offered it citywide in the 2022 general election. In the 2021 pilot, 4,082 packages were approved, 3,558 returned, and 3,515 tabulated; those tabulated ballots were 29% of all 12,102 ballots ([City Clerk's 2021 program report](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2021/ex/bgrd/backgroundfile-170671.pdf)). The distinctions among an application, a returned package, an accepted package, a tabulated ballot, and a valid candidate vote are therefore empirically necessary, not merely terminological.

The repeated recent pattern is a release on the day after advance voting closes, but this is an observation, not a guaranteed 2026 publication schedule. Official candidate-by-mode results are later: the City posts unofficial results only after polls close, then certifies results and publishes poll-by-poll mode data. In 2022 certification occurred October 27, three days after election day; final eligible-voter statistics followed later ([2022 certification release](https://www.toronto.ca/news/toronto-city-clerk-certifies-2022-toronto-municipal-election-results/)).

## Do polls identify respondents who already voted?

Three primary-source checks show the problem; they do not establish that every private questionnaire used the same wording.

- **Ipsos, June 9–13, 2023:** fieldwork overlapped all but the first day of advance voting. The detailed tables classify turnout as “Absolutely certain,” “Very likely,” “Not likely,” or “Won't vote,” then ask whom the respondent would vote for if the by-election “were held today.” There is no already-voted response category or published already-voted candidate stratum ([release and field dates](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2023-06/22-003004-01%20GN%20Media%20Release%20%283%29.pdf), [detailed tables](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2023-06/Toronto%20Mayoral%20Race%20Tables%202.pdf)).

- **Liaison, June 24–25, 2023:** the pollster's release says advance polls were in and many mail ballots had been cast, but publishes only “all voters” and “decided voters” toplines. The public release does not disclose an already-voted split ([Liaison release](https://press.liaisonstrategies.ca/bailao-surging-but-chow-remains-in-lead/)).

The same issue predates mail voting. Ipsos fielded October 21–23, 2014, after advance voting ended October 19, but asked how committed respondents were to vote if the election were tomorrow and how certain they were of the candidate they would support “on October 27th, Election Day.” Its public instrument has no already-voted category ([Ipsos release](https://www.ipsos.com/en-ca/john-tory-42-headed-toronto-mayors-chain-office-over-ford-31-chow-25), [detailed tables](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/publication/2014-10/6648-tb1.pdf)).

Consequently, a topline fielded after voting begins may combine two electorally different populations without making the mixture observable: people whose ballots are immutable and people whose eventual votes remain prospective. A poll's wording must be retained verbatim; “would vote,” “will vote,” “voted for,” and a combined “voted or certain” screen are not interchangeable.

## Factual constraints on the forecast target

For candidate \(c\), after some ballots have been cast, the final valid-vote count is an identity:

\[
V^{\text{final}}_c = V^{\text{already cast}}_c + V^{\text{not yet cast}}_c,
\qquad
s^{\text{final}}_c = \frac{V^{\text{final}}_c}{\sum_j V^{\text{final}}_j}.
\]

The City can reveal the size of part of the first stratum without revealing its candidate composition. That leads to five implementation-independent constraints:

1. **An observed advance-turnout count constrains volume, not candidate share.** Daily strike-off lists may reveal names and voting locations to campaigns, but neither they nor a public aggregate disclose ballot choice.

2. **Events after a ballot is cast cannot change that ballot.** An endorsement, withdrawal-related fact, debate, or poll release after advance voting can affect only votes not yet cast. A pooled poll may still move because its sample composition or answers move, but the already-cast electoral component is fixed.

3. **Historical validation must be cutoff-faithful.** A fold may use 115,911 after the October 15, 2022 release, 4,374 after the November 27, 2023 release, or 4,019 after the October 28, 2024 release. Substituting the later 115,899, 4,373, or 4,015 leaks post-cutoff revisions. Using final candidate-by-mode results is more serious leakage because it reveals the previously hidden composition.

4. **Count semantics must be explicit.** At minimum, data need to distinguish applications, packages issued, packages returned, ballots accepted/tabulated, `Number Voted`, and candidate-valid votes. In the [2023 mayoral report](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/8bb4-Final-for-web-2023-Mayor-ByElection-Report.pdf), the stated mode counts—28,143 mail, 129,745 advance, and 566,750 election day—sum to 724,638, while the same report says 725,333 ballots were cast. The [certified declaration](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/900e-Declaration-of-Results-for-the-2023-Toronto-By-Election-for-Mayor.pdf) candidate totals sum to 724,638. The official labels therefore do not support treating every turnout number as a valid-vote denominator.

5. **Availability has geography and timestamp.** A citywide count may precede a ward breakdown; candidate-only strike-off data are not public data; a date-only release cannot safely inform an earlier timestamp on that date. Each historical observation needs `known_at`, publication audience, geography, mode, preliminary/final status, and count definition.

These constraints leave open whether the eventual forecast represents already-cast and future ballots explicitly, approximately, or only through another validated formulation. They do establish what was observable at each Analysis Cutoff and what the final valid-vote-share target actually contains.
