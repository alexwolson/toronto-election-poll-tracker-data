# Calibrate the endpoint concentration to the winning-margin PIT, not to squared point error

ADR 0041 configured the reliability gate and the firm-balanced bridge qualified. A from-first-principles interrogation of the model then diagnosed that the bridge is **uniformly over-dispersed**: on the six regular cycles its winning-margin 80% band half-width is ≈2.4× the mean absolute margin error (a calibrated near-Gaussian band would sit near 1.6×), and its central-80% margin interval covers all six realizations. The cause is the concentration estimator itself. `_fit_concentration` set one global Dirichlet concentration by method of moments — matching the model's simplex spread to the mean squared point error across training cycles — and that squared error **includes the systematic polling bias** (the point is off-centre by ≈0.06 on average from house effects, turnout-model error, and undecided allocation). Absorbing bias into the variance term inflates the denominator, deflates κ, and widens every draw. The moment match cannot see its own defect: re-deriving κ by the same estimator on held-out training data pushes it *wider still* (it reads the bias as more variance to cover), so the over-width is structural, not a mis-fit.

Two candidate structural fixes were ruled out first, by measurement rather than argument. A **lead-dependent κ(t)** — the convergence a random-walk or Gaussian-process trajectory model would encode natively — was rejected because a drift-vs-systematic decomposition found no drift structure to recover: over-coverage is uniform across the 12/7/3/1-day lead grid (not concentrated at short lead), the miss does not correct as the poll nears (corr(err@L12, err@L1)=+0.73, mean|err@L1|/mean|err@L12|=0.95), and the floor+slope fit has an essentially zero, wrong-signed drift term. Municipal poll misses are locked in weeks out; a lead-flat concentration is vindicated. A **decoupled margin dispersion** was also rejected as unnecessary: tightening the whole simplex *improves* the guard metrics (winner log-score, share CRPS) rather than breaking them, so there is nothing to protect by decoupling. (The nonparametric, Gaussian-process, and hidden-Markov error models raised in the same interrogation were rejected earlier — the pooled margin error is light-tailed and featureless, and n=6 cannot identify sequence dynamics.)

The decision is to keep the moment match as an **anchor** and correct it with a single calibration scale: `κ = s · κ_moment`, `s ∈ [0.5, 4]`, chosen so the training winning-margin PIT dispersion (mean |PIT − 0.5|) matches the uniform value 0.25. An over-wide forecast pushes the realized margin toward the predictive centre, so its PIT dispersion falls below 0.25 (observed ≈0.15) and tightening raises it; the objective is **self-limiting** — it stops at calibration and cannot over-tighten past it — and is **guarded**, capping back to the anchor if tightening would degrade share-CRPS beyond 2%. The scale is fit per fold on training cycles only, so the qualification LOOCV and the reliability gate stay leakage-free, and it never sees the target lead, so the fit remains lead-flat.

The objective is **margin PIT dispersion specifically, not a proper score**, and that choice is the anti-tuning discipline here. Every proper score — CRPS, log-score, Brier — is also a reliability-gate metric (ADR 0041), so fitting κ to any of them would be circular with the gate it must then clear. PIT dispersion is a pure *calibration* target, orthogonal to the gate's proper scores: we fix the diagnosed defect (over-dispersion) and let the qualification ladder independently judge whether fixing calibration improved the forecasts. It is not tuned to the scoreboard, and the scoreboard's frozen maxima are untouched.

The consequence is that the endpoint **still qualifies, by more**. The fitted concentration rises from ≈34–38 to ≈90–120 on the over-wide folds and *loosens* the already-tight drop-2003 fold (93→77), collapsing the cross-fold κ range from 2.8× to 1.6× and so also damping the LOOCV concentration instability noted during the interrogation. On the regular population the bridge now beats the comparator on the primary winning-margin CRPS (0.03585 vs 0.04001, five of six cycles improved) and clears every comparator-anchored reliability maximum with more room than before — winner:log_score 0.11452, binary:close_result:brier 0.05061, shares:mean_candidate_crps 0.00436, against the frozen 0.241002 / 0.074634 / 0.005014. The maxima did **not** move: ADR 0041 froze them as literals (per ADR 0005), so the bar stayed fixed while the bridge cleared it further. The recalibration flows through **both** the bridge and the latest-sample comparator — both fit their concentration with the same estimator — so the relative comparison stays fair; and because the recalibrated comparator is itself tighter (its winning-margin CRPS falls from 0.04111 to 0.04001 on the regular population, its winner:log_score from 0.187679 to 0.15812), re-deriving the maxima from it would only *lower* them. Clearing the frozen, pre-0042 maxima is therefore the conservative choice, not a re-tuned threshold. (One honest wrinkle: after the recalibration the bridge's share-CRPS, 0.00436, edges just above the now-tighter comparator's 0.00418 while still well under the frozen 0.005014 maximum — the absolute gate is against the frozen number, and shares are not part of the relative arm, so qualification is unaffected.) The live effect on the 2026 forecast is modest and in the predicted direction: the Chow win probability rises 0.5963→0.6073, the projected-share bands narrow ≈0.3–0.5 point on each side, and `model_snapshot.json` (wards, council, ward win probabilities) is byte-identical because that layer does not consume the endpoint.

The case rests on the honest signals, not the loudest one. The winner log-score improvement (0.16546→0.11452) is **discounted**: all six regular cycles were won by the poll leader, so in a zero-upset sample any increase in winner confidence flatters the log-score, and it would score badly the first time an upset lands. The trustworthy movers are the margin CRPS (≈5%), close-result Brier (≈10%), and share CRPS (≈7%) — all improved, none confounded by the upset base rate. At n=6 realized coverage cannot independently confirm the tightening is calibrated (nothing misses until the scale is pushed past ×4), and the PIT target is itself noisy at this sample, which is why the scale is bounded and guarded and why the qualification ladder, not the calibration statistic, remains the arbiter.
