# Council candidate historical-hint flags

The Council v1 race card (ADR 0043) gains a per-candidate **historical-hint** signal: for each 2026 registered councillor candidate, which of the `defeatability-index` catalog's supported hints trigger, attached with the catalog's ready `frontend_copy`, effect estimate, and evidence tier. These are **historical frontend context only — never predictions, causal effects, or model inputs** — and sit alongside the existing exposure triggers and competitiveness facts as another strictly non-predictive cue.

**The hint definitions are consumed, not reinvented.** The 12 schema-2.1 public hints and their exact trigger conditions come from the sibling analysis and are vendored into `data/raw/hints/` (`supported_historical_hints.csv` + `historical_hint_contract.json`) by `scripts/sync_historical_hints.py`, re-synced when the analysis is refreshed. This repo computes only the **2026 per-candidate features** (the historical frame is 2003–2025, so the live field is not in it) following that contract, and fires the hints from the catalog's own `trigger_field`/`trigger_operator`. Diagnostic-only rows never enter the public feed.

**Identity: a deliberate, maintainer-chosen divergence from the contract.** The contract's first rule is "use the persistent `person_id`; never match by name." That rule protects the *analysis* — a mis-identification would corrupt the association estimates. But the live 2026 field is not in the canonical and carries no `person_id` (`councillor_registered.csv` is ward + names only), so on this side some name bridge is unavoidable. The chosen resolution, more generous than upstream by explicit instruction: a registration resolves to a **single upstream `person_id`** by a token-set match that tolerates accents, word order, and middle names, across *all* offices — but its history is then exactly the candidacies upstream linked to that `person_id`. We do **not** re-discover a person by sweeping same-named candidacies (which would merge people upstream deliberately kept apart and pull in `person_id`-blank rows), and a name that resolves to two different `person_id`s is left unmatched rather than guessed. The accepted trade-off is a possible false-positive link to a same-named person; for a non-predictive context cue that is low-stakes, and the failure mode is dominated by *under*-matching (ambiguous → no flag), not over-matching. This generosity is why a candidate whose only prior office was trustee, MPP, MP, or mayor — invisible to the councillor-only biography match — still gets flags (e.g. former MP Ted Opitz, four-term trustee Joseph Martino).

**History scope follows contract 2.1 exactly.** Aggregate history is every confirmed Candidacy before the subject Contest, including councillor, mayor, trustee, MP, and MPP races, whether won or lost. A hint may instead name one Office type explicitly. All-races-except-council and conditional council-history unions are ineligible because their counts contradict the complete history shown to readers. A Returning councillor is a prior council winner who is not the ward's sitting incumbent. The non-incumbent/non-returning findings cover any and multiple previous races, any prior win, the most recent race's signed margin and win status, a prior MPP race, and both required comparisons for an unsuccessful prior council run. The all-regime trustee-win hint remains public. Incumbents may receive the strongest-opponent recent-margin finding when every opponent's history is confirmed. Open contests may receive the two explicitly small-sample Returning-councillor findings. There is no public zero-wins or per-additional-victory hint.

**Consequence.** The council race-card schema is v5. The contract-2.1 migration expands the public catalog from two to 12 rows; it touches no forecast, no gate, and no model input, and the Council card remains outside the qualification ladder (ADR 0043).

**Reader-facing provenance.** The catalog's static `frontend_copy` is not the display contract. Each fired hint carries structured provenance: the relevant prior race and result, counts where needed, and the opponent's name for opponent-history findings. Binary findings may carry a positive or negative arrow. Continuous margin findings deliberately carry no arrow: the analysis establishes that stronger prior results are associated with more vote share, but it does not supply a candidate-level cutoff separating “positive” from “negative,” so zero must not be invented as that cutoff. Their source result is already present in the visible race history and is not repeated as a verdict. The paired unsuccessful-council-run rows remain together in the feed, but the ward cards omit both at the editor's direction. Overlapping rows are composed into one concise visible fact; a win count subsumes the general race count, and a sole named-office race uses the office-specific explanation. Opponent-history findings are computed for the whole field, including candidates whose own history is unresolved, and any displayed binary finding appears once at race level rather than beneath linked candidates. None of these directions conveys magnitude, probability, causality, or a forecast.
