# Re-point onto the normalized canonical schema; adopt person_id identity

The canonical dataset (`toronto-election-results`) was rebuilt from two flat, Toronto-only result files into a **normalized multi-table schema** — `election_events`, `contests`, `electoral_districts`, `election_results`, `candidacy_person_links`, `office_tenures`, `people`, `parties` — and its coverage expanded from mayoral+councillor to **all Toronto offices**: city council (mayor, councillor), school-board trustees (TDSB, TCDSB, two French boards), provincial (MPP), and federal (MP), 2003–2026, 5,488 result rows. The two flat files this repo vendored (`toronto_election_results.csv`, `council_composition.csv`) no longer exist, so `scripts/sync_canonical_results.py` and both readers were broken for refresh. This ADR re-points the pipeline onto the new schema, adopts the canonical persistent `person_id` as the identity key — closing the join-key deferral ADR 0044 explicitly left open — and vendors the full all-offices `election_results.csv`.

**What is vendored.** `sync_canonical_results.py` now vendors the single denormalized fact table `data/raw/canonical/election_results.csv` (a strict superset of the retired flat file: same votes, shares, `elected`, `acclaimed`, `incumbent`, plus `person_id`/`candidacy_id`, party, district, turnout, tenure). All offices are vendored and loadable; **only mayor and councillor are consumed** — trustee/MPP/MP rows are available for future work but drive no forecast (out of scope). `council_composition.csv` was dropped (it was vendored-but-unread; council incumbency comes from `ward_defeatability.csv`).

**Identity is now the canonical's, not a name-derived slug.** Both readers key on `person_id`. The mayoral outcome builder (`build_mayoral_outcome_rows`) filters `represented_body=toronto_city_council, office_type=mayor` and emits `person_id` where present, falling back to the always-present, rebuild-stable `candidacy_id` for the 49 unlinked minor candidates that need an id for the outcome table and the Monte-Carlo seed. The council biography reader (`load_council_results`) filters `office_type=councillor`, maps `boundary_regime`→`44-ward`/`25-ward` and `official_district_id` (`ward-N`)→`N`, and keys on `person_id`; rows the canonical could not link (228 councillor, all non-winners — every one of the 232 councillor winners is linked, so incumbent biographies are intact) carry a blank id and are skipped for biographies, exactly as before. The retired name-slug machinery (`_candidate_id`, `_KNOWN_CANDIDATE_IDS`, `_KNOWN_CANDIDATE_NAMES`) is deleted; the incumbent map (`_INCUMBENT_BY_CYCLE`) now holds Miller's and Tory's `person_id`s.

**Upstream linkage was completed first, by our request.** Several polled candidates who actually ran had a blank `person_id` in the pre-fix canonical — notably **George Smitherman** (2010 runner-up), plus Mark Saunders, Giorgio Mammoliti, Rob Davis, Sarah Climenhaga, and Blake Acton (2022–23). Making `person_id` the join key while those were blank would have dropped their poll readings and regressed coverage, so the gaps were fixed in the canonical (each candidacy linked to its existing person record; Rob Davis disambiguated and linked by the identity owner) before re-pointing here. The residual blanks (49 mayoral / 228 councillor) are genuine non-polled, non-winner minors and were, by decision, **not** chased upstream; the `candidacy_id` fallback (mayoral) and skip (council) handle them.

**The poll corpus was re-keyed to match the outcome, cycle by cycle.** `historical_mayoral/poll_responses.csv` candidate ids were regenerated by matching each response to the id the **outcome** assigns for that candidate in the reading's own cycle — accent- and punctuation-insensitive on the name, with a unique token-subset fallback for middle-name variations. This makes poll ids equal outcome ids by construction, whether the outcome used `person_id` or the `candidacy_id` fallback, and is robust to two failure modes a global name→`person_id` map hit during development: a candidate unlinked in one cycle but linked in another (Chloe-Marie Brown, blank `person_id` in 2022 → correctly keyed to her 2022 `candidacy_id`), and diacritic/spelling drift (Gil Peñalosa vs the canonical's *Penalosa*; Chloe Brown vs *Chloe-Marie Brown*). Non-runners polled only as hypotheticals (Stintz, Soknacki, a pre-withdrawal Rob Ford in 2014, etc.) match nothing and stay ineligible exactly as before.

**A latent slug bug surfaced and was fixed by the migration.** Ari Goldkind (a real 2014 candidate) had been silently dropped from eligibility because the retired scheme keyed the 2014 outcome as `toronto_2014:ari-goldkind` but the poll as `goldkind` — a mismatch. Under `person_id` he links correctly, which makes the fuller four-way Sep-22 Forum reading (Ford/Tory/Chow/Goldkind) eligible where only the three-way round was before; the selector rightly prefers it. This is the name-matching fragility `person_id` adoption is meant to remove.

**Qualification holds.** With inputs, per-cycle coverage, and verdict unchanged, the endpoint still **qualifies**: winner:log_score **0.1159**, close-result Brier **0.0489**, share CRPS **0.0043** — each under its frozen comparator-anchored maximum (0.2410 / 0.0746 / 0.0050). The small drift from the ADR-0044 figures (0.1135 / 0.0520 / 0.0043) is the expected reseed of the id-hashed Monte-Carlo seed, now keyed on canonical `person_id`/`candidacy_id` rather than slugs. The relaxed standard of ADR 0044 — identical inputs and coverage, identical verdict, metrics differing only by seeded-MC reshuffle — is met, and the deferred join-key adoption it named is now closed for both offices.

**Frontend feed.** Unchanged from ADR 0044: `model_snapshot.json` / `polls_snapshot.json` remain stale until the M3 layer is wired to an emitter (the INT milestone). This ADR touches only how results and identity are sourced.
