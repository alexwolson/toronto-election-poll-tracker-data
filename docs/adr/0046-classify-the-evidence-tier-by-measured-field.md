# Classify the mayoral evidence tier by measured field, not fieldwork date

The mayoral evidence tier (ADR 0033) gated a current-cycle poll as post-Final by its **fieldwork date**: a sample counted only when its fieldwork interval reached or passed the conservative moment the certified Final Ballot became publicly knowable (nomination close, ADR 0002). This ADR replaces that date boundary with a **field-consistency** test: a sample is **final-field** when the Final Ballot is certified *and* the named candidates it individually measured equal the certified viable field — the candidates the forecast models distinctly.

**Why.** The date boundary was only ever a proxy for the property that actually matters: *did this poll measure the field that will be on the ballot?* When the field settles well before nomination close — as in 2026, where Chow, Bradford, and Alexander have been the stable field for months — the proxy withholds needlessly: polls already in hand measure the real final field, yet count for nothing until a poll happens to be *dated* after close. Replacing the proxy with the property it stood for lets those polls qualify the moment the field is certified, without waiting for a fresh post-close poll that would measure the same thing.

**It is a strict generalization, not a loosening.** A post-close poll measuring the certified field is still final-field; the date is now incidental rather than decisive. What the field test *adds* is pre-close polls that measured the certified field; what it correctly *keeps out* is stronger than the date rule ever was — a poll missing a viable candidate (fielded before that candidate entered — the pre-Alexander polls) or carrying a candidate absent from the final ballot (fielded before they withdrew — the early-2026 polls measuring Tory/Furey/Ford) is not final-field, where the bare date test would have admitted any post-close poll regardless of the field it measured.

**It self-protects.** The test is evaluated against the *certified* field, so it cannot run ahead of the ballot. Before certification the viable field is unknown (`final_field` is None), no sample is final-field, and the cycle caps at M1 — correctly withholding before nominations close. If a surprise candidate files at close, the certified field contains someone no existing poll measured, nothing is final-field, and the tier falls back to M1 until a poll measuring the new field lands. The property that made the date rule safe — never forecasting a provisional field — is preserved exactly.

**Consequence for 2026.** Against the current poll record, three settled-field samples measure exactly {Chow, Bradford, Alexander} — the Jul 29 Forum wave and the Aug 5 and Aug 20 Liaison waves (three samples, two pollsters). Once nominations close and confirm that field, they are final-field, lifting the cycle to **M3** and clearing the tier floor for every mayoral quantity (Close-Result and Incumbent-Defeat at M2; Candidate Win at M3 for Bradford and Alexander, each measured in all three). The tier floor is only one gate: publication still requires the Mandatory Sensitivity Variants (produced by snapshot integration, INT) and a passing Band Stability Gate (ADR 0018).

**Labels.** M2 and M3 are renamed "Final-Field Polling" and "Replicated Final-Field Polling"; the earlier "Post-Final" wording is temporal and would misdescribe a pre-close poll that measured the certified field. M0 (Structural Only) and M1 (Pre-Final Polling) are unchanged.

**Gate version and history — one open item.** This is a gate change, so `MAYORAL_GATE_REGISTRY_VERSION` is bumped (ADR 0005). The historical unlock counts (M2 in 7 cycles, M3 in 6) are **retained pending a recompute**, not proven unchanged. The recompute is non-monotonic: field-consistency *adds* any pre-close poll that measured the cycle's viable field and *drops* any post-close poll that measured only a subset of it, so a count could move in either direction. It is expected to stay well clear of the three-cycle floor — every competitive historical cycle had late polls measuring its main field — but that is a reasonable expectation, not a verified result. A rigorous per-cycle recompute needs each historical cycle's certified viable field, which is not yet assembled; it is a documented follow-up, and until it runs the retained 7/6 are the frozen literals the gate uses.

**Scope.** This changes a publication-eligibility layer, not the model: the endpoint's qualification is unaffected. It does bind **INT**: the live poll selection feeding the endpoint must use the same field-consistency rule, so the forecast and the tier it publishes under agree on which polls are final-field. The `final_field` input itself is the certified viable field, supplied by INT's live-target assembly.
